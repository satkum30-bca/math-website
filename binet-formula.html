Binet's Formula

Go Back

How do we go about finding a general formula for the Fibonacci Sequence? We have the Fibonacci-Q matrix, and we know that exponentiating it yields Fibonacci Numbers. If we can find a general formula for Qn, we then have a general formula for the Fibonacci Numbers.

Linear algebra gives us the tools to do this through diagonalization. We obtain the two eigenvalues of Q are:

φ = 1 + 5 2 , ψ = 1 − 5 2

We then get:

Q = [ φ ψ 1 1 ] [ φ 0 0 ψ ] 15 [ 1 −ψ −1 φ ]

Then:

Qn = [ φ ψ 1 1 ] [ φn 0 0 ψn ] 15 [ 1 −ψ −1 φ ] = 15 [ φn+1 ψn+1 φn ψn ] [ 1 −ψ −1 φ ]

Looking at the bottom left entry of this matrix, we arrive at:

Fn = φn − ψn 5

This is known as Binet's formula for the Fibonacci sequence. We can simplify this formula, though. Since | ψ | < 1 , the ψn term will become extremely small. So, we can rewrite the expression as:

Fn = { φn 5 }

where {⋅} is the round function.

This image shows us that the exponential function φn 5 is a very close approximation for the Fibonacci sequence. This also explains why the ratio between two consecutive terms of the Fibonacci sequence approaches the golden ratio!
